# Cardiac Signals Lab demo

This is a static, browser-only demonstration of two proposed mixed-reality social decision tasks. It is designed to make the grant's behavioral outcomes concrete: what the participant sees, what they decide before and after a cardiac cue, and which variables can be compared across incentive and signal-access conditions.

**Live demo:** https://georgefejer91.github.io/cardiac-signals-lab-demo/

The interface uses a transparent, passthrough-first canvas: only physical cards and translucent research controls are rendered, so the task can sit over a mixed-reality camera or WebXR passthrough layer without a synthetic room or tabletop. The ordinary GitHub Pages preview shows the same transparent composition in a standard browser.

It automatically switches to a touch-optimized phone layout on narrow screens. It uses device-safe viewport sizing, notch and home-indicator safe areas, stacked task panels, and touch targets of at least 44 pixels. Both complete game sequences are tested at a 390 × 844 smartphone viewport as well as on desktop.

## Cardiac display modes

Every cardiac panel has four switchable views of the same underlying simulated signal:

- **ECG:** a moving single-lead waveform generated reproducibly with NeuroKit2's ECGSYN model, plus BPM, beat interval, and synthetic RMSSD.
- **Pulse:** a minimal beat-timed pulse and rate display.
- **Affect:** an Affect Tracker–inspired activation lens derived from rate change and variability.
- **Heart:** a large heart-shaped beat display with compact metrics.

The included profiles are generated by `scripts/generate_neurokit_profiles.py` with NeuroKit2 0.2.13 and fixed random seeds. They are synthetic demonstration data, not clinical recordings. The activation display is deliberately labelled as an unvalidated interface proxy: heart rate alone does not identify excitement, valence, emotion, deception, or concealed information.

## Playable paradigms

### Joint Discrimination

The participant plays **Player B**, the less-informed judge. They see two candidate shapes and an exactly ambiguous target printed on realistic physical stimulus cards. Simulated Player A sees a more diagnostic target, then sends **A**, **B**, or **Pass**. Player B makes a private initial choice and confidence rating before the signal, then a private final choice and confidence rating after it.

Neither numeric confidence rating is shown to Player A. The demo stores initial/final accuracy, choice revision, signal uptake, confidence, and payoff internally, but deliberately gives no trial-by-trial correctness or strategy feedback in the participant view. Cooperative incentives make Player A truthful. The mixed-motive script includes truth telling, bluffing, and withholding.

This is a nonverbal experimental adaptation of Pulford, Mangiarulo, and Colman's dyadic shape-comparison task. Their studies used open discussion followed by private written decisions and private confidence ratings. The partner could hear spoken confidence and observe response timing, but did not receive the numeric rating. The original task did not include a pre-signal response, A/B/Pass interface, or visible heart rate; these are proposed extensions.

### Concealed Information

A scripted Player 1 privately recognizes one standard playing card among four candidates (7♥, Q♠, 4♦, and 9♣). The participant plays the **observer**, records a private initial card choice and confidence, then sees a neutral buffer followed by every candidate in a timed probe sequence. A live, replayed, or hidden cardiac panel appears beside the probes. The observer then records a private final choice and confidence. Player 1 never sees those responses during the trial, and the target is not revealed during the session.

The demo stores prior/final accuracy, belief revision, confidence, payoff, and prior-to-posterior Brier-score improvement internally. In the live competitive condition, the scripted cardiac response includes a target-contingent deceleration; replay moves the same plausible dip to a non-target card, breaking trial-level contingency.

This is a dyadic, four-alternative adaptation of Klein Selle et al.'s card-based Concealed Information Test. Their original task was **not a two-player guessing game**: one instrumented participant selected one of six cards, self-selected conceal or reveal, and then viewed a buffer, the critical item, four controls, and a catch item with long interstimulus intervals. Adding an observer, showing that observer the participant's cardiac activity, collecting pre/post beliefs, and manipulating dyadic incentives are the grant's proposed extensions. The accelerated demo is an implementation preview, not an exact reproduction of the published timing.

## Experimental factors

- **Incentives:** cooperative versus mixed-motive in Joint Discrimination; cooperative versus competitive in Concealed Information.
- **Cardiac access:** trial-contingent live signal, plausible noncontingent replay, or hidden display.
- **Primary behavioral logic:** compare pre-signal and post-signal accuracy and probability judgments.
- **Interpretation boundary:** the display is an experimental social cue, not an emotion decoder, lie detector, or direct readout of a card.

All partner behavior and physiology are simulated. The demo records, stores, and transmits no participant data.

## Local development

Requirements: Node.js 22.13 or newer.

    npm install
    npm run dev

The local site opens at `http://localhost:3000/`.

Validation and static GitHub Pages export:

    npm run lint
    npm run build
    npm run build:pages

`npm run build` validates the OpenAI Sites/Vinext application. `npm run build:pages` writes the static deployment to `out/`.
